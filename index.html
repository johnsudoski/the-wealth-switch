How It Works

Brainwave entrainment is the idea that your brain can gradually sync its activity to a repeated sound pattern — a phenomenon called the frequency following response. When two very slightly different tones play separately into each ear, your brain perceives a third "beat" equal to the difference between them.

Research in this area is real, but mixed. A meta-analysis published in Psychological Research (Schwarz & Fuchs, 2018) reviewed roughly 22 studies and found a medium, consistent effect on cognition, anxiety, and pain perception. Other systematic reviews have found no measurable change in EEG power for some frequency ranges. In short: the phenomenon has genuine scientific interest, but it doesn't work identically for everyone, and short daily sessions aren't a guaranteed cognitive upgrade.

What we can say with more confidence: spending a few focused minutes a day in a quiet space, breathing intentionally and listening to calming audio, is a legitimate relaxation practice on its own — whatever additional benefit specific frequencies provide is the part that's still being studied.
